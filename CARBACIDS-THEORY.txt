## **CARBOXYLIC ACID AND ITS DERIVATIVES**

### **1.0 INTRODUCTION - CARBOXYLIC ACID**

Organic compounds having -COOH group are called as Carboxylic acids. This functional group is composed of
Carbonyl ($-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\text{C}}}-$) and hydroxyl (—OH) group.

$\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\text{C}}}- + -OH \rightarrow -\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\text{C}}}-OH$
Carbonyl group $\quad$ Hydroxyl group $\quad\quad$ Carboxylic group

The properties of the carboxylic group are not simply the combined properties of these two groups, but it has its own distinctive properties. The acidic nature of carboxylic acids is due to the presence of replaceable H-atom in the Carboxylic group. The general formula is $C_nH_{2n}O_2$.

### **1.1 CLASSIFICATION**
**1. Monocarboxylic acid (RCOOH)**
Having one carboxylic group, also called monobasic acid. General formula - $C_nH_{2n}O_2$ (n= 1, 2, 3, ........). Higher mono carboxylic acids are called **fatty acids**.
**Example :** $CH_3COOH$ acetic acid

**2. Dicarboxylic acid**
Having two carboxylic groups, also called dibasic acid.
**Example :** $COOH-COOH$ Oxalic acid

**3. Tricarboxylic acid**
Having three carboxylic groups also called tribasic acid.
**Example :** $HO-\overset{CH_2COOH}{\underset{CH_2COOH}{\underset{|}{\text{C}}}}-COOH$ Citric acid

$R-CH_2OH + [O] \xrightarrow{KMnO_4/H^+} RCHO + H_2O \xrightarrow{[O]} RCOOH$
$K_2Cr_2O_7/H^+$

**2. By oxidation of aldehydes**
Aldehydes on oxidation with usual oxidizing agent gives carboxylic acid with same number of carbon atoms as in the aldehyde.
$R-CHO + [O] \xrightarrow{\text{Tollen's Reagent}} R-COO^{\ominus} \xrightarrow{H^{\oplus}} R-COOH$
$C_6H_5CHO + [O] \rightarrow C_6H_5COO^{\ominus} \rightarrow C_6H_5COOH$

**3. By oxidation of Alkenes**
(a) $RCH=CHR' \xrightarrow{\text{(i) conc. } KMnO_4 / OH^{\ominus} \text{ heat}} \xrightarrow{\text{(ii) } H_3O^+} RCOOH + R'COOH$
(b) $RCH=CHR' \xrightarrow{\text{(i) } O_3} \xrightarrow{\text{(ii) } Zn/H_2O, \text{Oxidative}} RCOOH + R'COOH$

**4. By Carboxylation of Grignard Reagent**
$R-Br \xrightarrow{Mg, \text{Dry ether}} RMgBr \xrightarrow{CO_2} R-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\text{C}}}-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_2O/H^+} R-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\text{C}}}-OH$

**5. By hydrolysis of derivatives of carboxylic acid**
$R-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\text{C}}}-Cl + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+ \text{ or } OH^-} R-COOH + HCl$
$(R-CO)_2O + H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+ \text{ or } OH^-} 2R-COOH$

### **1.3 PHYSICAL PROPERTIES OF CARBOXYLIC ACID**
These are polar substances and can form H-bonds with each other to form dimer structures.
$R-O-H \cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot O=C(OH)-R$
$R-(HO)C=O \cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot H-O-R$

**1. Boiling Point**
Due to dimeric structure, the effective molecular mass of the acid becomes double the actual mass. Hence Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of comparable molecular masses.

**2. Solubility**
Due to hydrogen bonding carboxylic acid show appreciable solubility in water. Its solubility in water is greater than alcohol because H-bonding strength is given in order of
Carboxylic acid > alcohol.

**3. Melting Point**
The melting point of the carboxylic acid with even number of carbon atoms is higher than acid with odd number of carbon atoms. Such effect is observed in first ten members of the homologous series. This feature is based on the fact that in the carboxylic acids with even number of carbon atoms, the terminal methyl group and carboxylic group are on the opposite sides of zig-zag carbon atom chain. Hence they fit better in the crystal lattice resulting in stronger inter molecular forces than the other acids acids with odd number of carbon atom have carboxyl and terminal methyl group on the same side of zig-zag carbon chain which result in poor fitting in the crystal lattice. This causes a weak forces among molecules and result for the relatively lower melting point.
The melting point are usually higher than those of aliphatic acid of comparable molecular masses. This is due to planar structure of benzene ring in the acid which can pack closely in the crystal than aliphatic acids.

### **1.4 CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF CARBOXYLIC ACID**
**1. Acidity of carboxylic acid**
Acidity is relative case with which it loses a proton leaving behind the anion. Its acid strength depends upon the difference in the stability of the acid and its anion.

$[R-\overset{:\ddot{O}:}{\overset{||}{C}}-\ddot{O}-H \leftrightarrow R-\overset{:\ddot{O}:^{\ominus}}{\overset{|}{\underset{\oplus}{C}}}-\ddot{O}-H] \rightleftharpoons H^{\oplus} + [R-\overset{:\ddot{O}:}{\overset{||}{C}}-\ddot{O}:^{\ominus} \leftrightarrow R-\overset{:\ddot{O}:^{\ominus}}{\overset{|}{\underset{\ominus}{C}}}-\ddot{O}:]$
non-equivalent structures of resonance hybrid
equivalent structures of resonance hybrid

both acid and its anion are stabilized by resonance, stabilization is far greater for the anion than for acid because anion gives two identical resonating structure.

There are two facts on which ease of decarboxylation depends.
**(i) When the carboxylate ion decarboxylate, it forms a resonance stabilized enolate ion.**
$R-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{C}}-CH_2-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{C}}-O^{\ominus} \xrightarrow{-CO_2} R-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{C}}-\overset{\ominus}{C}H_2 \leftrightarrow R-\overset{O^{\ominus}}{\overset{|}{\text{C}}}=CH_2$
Resonance stabilized enolate anion
Due to the resonance enolate ion is much more stable than normal carbanion.

$[R-C(=O)-CH_2-C(=O)-OH \text{ cyclic transition state}] \xrightarrow{\Delta} [R-C(OH)=CH_2] + CO_2$
$\xrightarrow{\text{Tautomerisation}} R-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{C}}-CH_3$

This is a general method for the conversion of alkanamides in to one carbon less primary amines. Ethanamide is heated with bromine and excess of KOH.
$CH_3CONH_2 + Br_2 + 4KOH \rightarrow CH_3NH_2 + K_2CO_3 + 2KBr + 2H_2O$

    **Step 1:** $CH_3-CONH_2 + Br_2 + KOH \rightarrow CH_3CONHBr + KBr + H_2O$

### **2.0 CHARACTERISTIC REACTION FOR ACID DERIVATIVES IS NUCLEOPHILIC SUBSTITUTION REACTION**
*   **Mechanism**
    $CH_3-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\underset{Z}{\underset{|}{C}}}} + Nu^{\ominus} \rightarrow CH_3-\overset{\large :O:^{\ominus}}{\underset{Z \quad Nu}{\underset{| \quad |}{C}}} \rightarrow CH_3-\overset{\large\text{O}}{\overset{||}{\underset{Nu}{\underset{|}{C}}}} + Z^{\ominus}$
    $sp^2$ hybrid C-atom $\quad$ $sp^3$ hybrid C-atom
    In this reaction Z is leaving group. Weak bases are good leaving groups.
    Reactivity order - depends on the basic Character of Z
    basicity : $Cl^- < CH_3COO^- < C_2H_5O^- < NH_2^-$
    In the given groups Cl$^-$ is the weakest base so it is the best leaving group.

*   **Reactivity order**
    $CH_3COCl > CH_3COOCOCH_3 > CH_3COOC_2H_5 > CH_3CONH_2$
    In acid derivatives the carbonyl group $>C=O$ is attached to highly electronegative $-Cl, CH_3COO-, -NH_2$ etc. group due to electron withdrawing effect of these groups, the electron density on the carbonyl carbon is reduced further. Thus acyl group is readily attacked by $\overset{\ominus}{Nu}$ shows nucleophilic substitution reaction.
