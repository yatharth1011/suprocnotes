## **CHEMICAL TESTS FOR FUNCTIONAL GROUPS**

*   Elemental analyses, physical, spectral and solubility test give a reasonable idea regarding the identity of unknown compound.
*   Despite the tremendous importance and ease of spectral analyses, chemical tests are indispensable to complete characterization.

**1. Test Of Acidic Hydrogen; Unsaturation; Terminal Alkyne; Alcohols; Phenol**

**(i) Sodium Metal**
$2ROH \text{ (alcohol)} + 2Na \rightarrow 2RO^{-}Na^{+} + H_2(g)$

*   **Positive Test:** evolution of gas
*   The rate is highly variable and depends upon the alcohol structure.
*   The sample must be water-free.
*   Other Functional groups that give positive Test:
    $R_2NH, RSH, RC \equiv C-H, RCO_2H$

**(ii) Ceric Ammonium Nitrate Oxidation (CAN Test)**

*   The reaction proceeds via the formation of a red complex which then oxidizes the alcohol, leading to the decolorization of the solution as Ce(IV) is reduced to Ce(III). The simplified reaction is:
    $(NH_4)_2Ce(NO_3)_6 \text{ (ceric ammonium nitrate, yellow)} + ROH \text{ (alcohol)} \rightarrow \text{Red complex} \rightarrow Ce(III) \text{ products (colour decolourise)}$

*   **Positive Test:** Color changes from yellow to red first then to colorless solution. (1 min. to 12 hrs)
*   A positive test includes successively the formation, and then the disappearance of the red color.
*   Very good test for $1^\circ$, $2^\circ$ alcohol, but slow for $3^\circ$ alcohol
*   **Note:** Phenols gives brown or black products.

**(iii) Jones Oxidation: Chromic anhydride or Chromium Trioxide ($CrO_3$)**

*   A positive test for $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$ alcohols consists in the changes from an orange-red color ($Cr^{6+}$) to opaque suspension with green to blue color ($Cr^{3+}$) in 2 sec.
*   $3^\circ$ alcohols give no visible reaction within 2 sec. remaining orange.
*   **Note:** Aldehydes give positive result.

    $3RCH_2OH \text{ (1° alcohol)} + 4CrO_3 \text{ (orange-red)} + 6H_2SO_4 \rightarrow 3 \underset{\text{carboxylic acid}}{R-\overset{\Large O}{\overset{||}{C}}-OH} + 9H_2O + 2Cr_2(SO_4)_3 \text{ (intense blue to green)}$

**(iv) Lucas Test (HCl/ZnCl$_2$)**

*   Substrates that easily give rise to cationic character at the carbon bearing the hydroxyl group undergo the Lucas test readily

    $R_2CHOH \text{ (2° alcohol)} + HCl \xrightarrow{ZnCl_2} R_2CHCl \text{ (alkyl halide)} + H_2O$

    $R_3COH \text{ (3° alcohol)} + HCl \xrightarrow{ZnCl_2} R_3CCl \text{ (alkyl halide)} + H_2O$

    **Positive Test:** Formation of insoluble layer or emulsion in 5-10 min

    $\underset{H_3C-C(CH_3)_2-OH}{\Large \text{tert-butyl alcohol}} \xrightarrow{H^+} \underset{H_3C-C(CH_3)_2-OH_2^+}{\Large \text{protonated alcohol}} \xrightarrow{-H_2O} \underset{H_3C-C^+(CH_3)_2}{\Large \text{tert-butyl cation}} \xrightarrow{Cl^-} \underset{H_3C-C(CH_3)_2-Cl}{\Large \text{tert-butyl chloride}}$

*   **Note:** Primary alcohols do not give positive result.

*   Since the Lucas test depends on the appearance of the alkyl chloride as a second liquid phase, it is normally applicable only to alcohols that are soluble in the reagent.

*   Allyl alcohol, although a primary alcohol, yields a carbocation that is relatively stable.

    $CH_2=CH-CH_2-OH \xrightarrow{H^+, -H_2O} [CH_2=CH-CH_2^+ \leftrightarrow ^+CH_2-CH=CH_2]$

*   Thus, allyl alcohol reacts rapidly with the Lucas reagent and the reaction is accompanied by the evolution of heat. Addition of ice water to the reaction results in the formation of the allyl chloride as a separate layer.

**(v) Periodic Acid ($HIO_4$) Test for detection of Vicinal Diols and Related Compounds**

*   $\underset{\text{vicinal diol}}{R-CH(OH)-CH(OH)-R} + HIO_4 \text{ (periodic acid)} \rightarrow 2 \underset{\text{aldehyde}}{R-CHO} + H_2O + HIO_3 \text{ (iodic acid)}$

*   $\underset{\text{1,2-diketone}}{R-CO-CO-R} + HIO_4 \text{ (periodic acid)} + H_2O \rightarrow 2 \underset{\text{carboxylic acid}}{R-COOH} + HIO_3 \text{ (iodic acid)}$

*   $\underset{\text{$\alpha$-hydroxy ketone}}{R-CH(OH)-CO-R'} + HIO_4 \text{ (periodic acid)} \rightarrow \underset{\text{carboxylic acid}}{R'-COOH} + \underset{\text{aldehyde}}{R-CHO} + HIO_3 \text{ (iodic acid)}$

*   $\underset{\text{$\alpha$-hydroxyaldehyde}}{R-CH(OH)-CHO} + HIO_4 \text{ (periodic acid)} \rightarrow \underset{\text{carboxylic acid}}{H-COOH} + \underset{\text{aldehyde}}{R-CHO} + HIO_3 \text{ (iodic acid)}$

*   $\underset{\text{$\beta$-hydroxyamine}}{R-CH(OH)-CH(R')-NHR'} + HIO_4 \text{ (periodic acid)} \rightarrow \underset{\text{aldehydes}}{R-CHO + R'-CHO} + HIO_3 + NH_3$

*   The iodic acid is detected with 5% $AgNO_3$ solution -an immediate precipitation of silver iodate occurs.

    $HIO_3 \text{ (iodic acid)} + AgNO_3 \text{ (silver nitrate)} \rightarrow HNO_3 + AgIO_3(s) \text{ (silver iodate (white))}$

*   Olefins, $2^\circ$ alcohols, 1,3-glycols, ketones and aldehydes are not affected by $HIO_4$ under the test condition.

**(vi) Acetyl Chloride**

*   **Positive Test:** Evolution of heat and HCl gas and formation of ester as a top layer

*   $ROH \text{ (alcohol)} + \underset{\text{acetyl chloride}}{CH_3COCl} \rightarrow \underset{\text{ester}}{CH_3COOR} + HCl(g)$

*   $ArOH \text{ (phenol)} + \underset{\text{acetyl chloride}}{CH_3COCl} \rightarrow \underset{\text{ester}}{CH_3COOAr} + HCl(g)$

*   $3^\circ$ alcohols form primarily alkyl chloride due to the reaction of the liberated HCl on another molecule of the alcohol.

*   Alkanes are not usually characterized chemically because they are quite inert to most classification reactions
*   Alkanes fall into solubility class I
*   Mostly, rely on physical and spectral characterization

**(i) Bromine in $CCl_4$**

*   $\underset{\text{alkene}}{C=C} + Br_2 \text{ (red-brown)} \rightarrow \underset{\text{1,2-dibromoalkane (colourless)}}{C(Br)-C(Br)}$
*   $\underset{\text{alkyne}}{-C \equiv C-} \xrightarrow{Br_2 \text{ (brown)}} \underset{\text{1,2-dibromoalkene}}{C(Br)=C(Br)} \xrightarrow{Br_2 \text{ (brown)}} \underset{\text{1,1,2,2-tetrabromoalkane (colourless)}}{C(Br)_2-C(Br)_2}$

**3. Tests for Phenols**
*   As for alcohols, the acidic hydrogen in phenol can be detected with sodium (hydrogen gas is evolved) or with acetyl chloride (an ester layer is formed).

    $ArOH \text{ (Phenol)} + \underset{\text{Acetyl chloride}}{CH_3COCl} \rightarrow \underset{\text{ester}}{CH_3COOAr} + HCl(g)$
*   Phenols undergo reaction with yellow ceric ammonium nitrate to produce brown or black products.

    $(NH_4)_2Ce(NO_3)_6 \text{ (ceric ammonium nitrate (yellow))} + ArOH \text{ (phenol)} \rightarrow \underset{\text{(brown or black)}}{(NH_4)_2Ce(NO_3)_5(OAr)} + HNO_3$
*   Phenols reduce potassium permanganate solution and undergo oxidation to quinones.
*   The manganese is reduced from +7 which gives a purple solution to +4 which is brown.

    $3 \underset{\text{Phenol}}{ArOH} + 4KMnO_4 \rightarrow 3 \underset{\text{quinone}}{O=C_6H_4=O} + 4MnO_2 \text{ (brown)} + 4KOH + H_2O$

**6. Tests for Alkyl Halides**
*   **Halogen analysis:**
    Aliphatic halides are often detected initially by qualitative Halogen analysis.

*   **Ethanolic Silver Nitrate & (ii) Sodium Iodide in Acetone**
    Both tests involve displacement of halogen:
    $AgNO_3$/ethanol test proceeds by a carbocation ($SN_1$) process NaI/acetone test proceeds by a direct displacement ($SN_2$).
    $AgNO_3$/ethanol test: $R_3CX > R_2CHX > RCH_2X$
    NaI/acetone test: $R_3CX < R_2CHX < RCH_2X$

**(i) Ethanolic Silver Nitrate Solution**
*   The reaction of alkyl halide with silver nitrate yields a silver halide precipitate
    $RX \text{ (alkyl halide)} + AgNO_3 \text{ (silver nitrate)} \xrightarrow{CH_3CH_2OH} AgX(s) \text{ (silver halide)} + RONO_2$
*   **Positive Test:** formation of ppt. Indicates $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ RX
*   $1^\circ$ RX, Ar-X, and vinyl halides give negative Result.
*   **Note:** allylic and benzylic RX give positive result
*   The identity of the halogen can sometimes be determined from the color of the silver halide: AgCl (white); AgBr (pale yellow); AgI (yellow)

**(ii) Sodium Iodide in Acetone Test**
*   The NaI test can be used to test for the presence of bromine or chlorine.
*   Sodium halide (NaX) precipitates from the solution.
    $\underset{\text{alkyl chloride}}{RCl} + NaI \xrightarrow{\text{acetone}} \underset{\text{alkyl iodide}}{RI} + NaCl(s) \text{ (sodium chloride)}$
    $\underset{\text{alkyl bromide}}{RBr} + NaI \text{ (sodium iodide)} \xrightarrow{\text{acetone}} \underset{\text{alkyl iodide}}{RI} + NaBr(s) \text{ (sodium bromide)}$
*   **Positive Test:** precipitate forms
*   **Indications:** $1^\circ$, $2^\circ$ RX, allylic and benzylic halides. Not good for ArX, vinyl halides, $CHCl_3$, and $3^\circ$ RX
*   Since reactivity toward alcoholic silver nitrate is often very different from reactivity toward sodium iodide in acetone, both tests should be used with any halogen compound.

**(iii) Hinsberg Test**
*   Based on the reaction of the amine with benzenesulfonyl chloride and can be used to distinguish among $1^\circ, 2^\circ, \text{and } 3^\circ$ amines.
*   $1^\circ$ amines: give solution that produce ppt after addition of HCl

**10. Tests for Aromatics**

*   If the molecule already contains reactive chemical substituents (acids, amines, ethers, carbonyl compounds, etc..) use the corresponding classification test for that particular group.

*   New substituents can be added onto the aromatic ring or existing substituents can be modified, such that the new **Compound may be more readily characterized.**

**(i) Fuming Sulfuric Acid**

*   $ArH \text{ (aromatic compound)} + H_2SO_4/SO_3 \rightarrow ArSO_3H \text{ (arylsulfonic acid)} + \text{heat}$

*   This test is good for aromatics with no other functional groups.

*   **Positive Test:** the aromatic compound dissolves completely in $H_2SO_4$ with the evolution of heat (Fuming).

**(ii) Chloroform and Aluminum Chloride**

*   $3ArH \text{ (aromatic compound)} + CHCl_3 \xrightarrow{AlCl_3} \underset{\text{triarylmethane}}{Ar_3CH} + 3HCl$

**(iii) Azoxybenzene and Aluminum Chloride**

*   The color of the solution or precipitate is dependent on the functional groups present on the aryl group.
*   Aromatic hydrocarbons and their halogen derivatives produce a deep orange to dark-red color in solution or give a precipitate.
*   Fused aromatic rings (such as naphthalene, anthracene, and phenanthrene) produce brown color).
*   Aliphatic hydrocarbons give no color or, at most, a pale yellow.

**11. Tests for Ethers**

*   **Caution:** ethers form extremely explosive peroxides upon standing, specially when exposed to air and/or light. Liquid ethers that show solid precipitates should not be handled.
*   Ethers are only a little more polar and slightly more reactive than either saturated hydrocarbons or alkyl halides.

**(i) Hydroiodic Acid (Zeisel's Alkoxyl method)**

*   $ROR + 2HI \rightarrow 2RI + H_2O$
*   $ArOR \text{ (ether)} + HI \text{ (hydroiodic acid)} \rightarrow ArOH \text{ (phenol)} + RI \text{ (alkyl iodide)}$
*   $Hg(NO_3)_2 \text{ (mercuric acetate)} + 2RI \xrightarrow{H_2O} \underset{\text{mercuric iodide (orange)}}{HgI_2}\downarrow + 2ROH + 2HNO_3$

*   **Positive Test:** Orange or Orange-red color indicating mercuric iodide.
*   **Note:** Ethyl and methyl esters give also positive result.

**(iii) Iodine Test for Ethers and Unsaturated Hydrocarbons**

*   $R_2O: + I_2 \leftrightarrow R_2O:I_2$
*   Alkene $+ I_2 \leftrightarrow \text{Alkene-}\pi\text{-complex-}I_2$

*   **Positive test:** the color of the solution changes from purple to tan.
*   Aromatic hydrocarbons, saturated hydrocarbons, fluorinated hydrocarbons and chlorinated hydrocarbons do not react.
*   Unsaturated hydrocarbons produce a light-tan solid, while retaining the purple color of the iodine solution.
